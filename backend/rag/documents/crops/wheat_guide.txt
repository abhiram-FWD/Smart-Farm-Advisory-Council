WHEAT CROP ADVISORY GUIDE

1. WATER MANAGEMENT

Wheat requires adequate soil moisture for healthy growth.
Water requirements vary according to soil type, climate,
crop variety and growth stage.

During critical growth stages, insufficient moisture can
reduce crop development and yield.

Before irrigation, consider current soil moisture,
recent rainfall and expected rainfall.

Avoid excessive irrigation because waterlogging can
damage roots and reduce soil aeration.

2. SOIL AND NUTRIENTS

Nitrogen supports leaf development and vegetative growth.
Phosphorus supports root development and energy transfer.
Potassium contributes to plant water regulation and stress tolerance.

Yellowing leaves may be associated with nutrient deficiency,
water stress, soil conditions or disease.

Symptoms alone cannot confirm a nutrient deficiency.
Soil testing is recommended before fertilizer decisions.

3. CROP HEALTH

Yellow leaves can have several possible causes,
including nutrient imbalance, water stress and disease.

Wilting may indicate insufficient water, root damage,
waterlogging or other environmental stress.

Leaf spots may indicate disease, but visual symptoms
alone are not sufficient for definitive diagnosis.

Inspect affected plants and monitor symptom progression.
Consult an agricultural expert when symptoms spread
or the cause remains uncertain.

4. FARM MONITORING

Record irrigation dates, rainfall, soil measurements,
crop growth stages and observed symptoms.

Compare new observations with previous farm records
to identify changes and recurring problems.

5. ADVISORY SAFETY

Recommendations should consider crop stage,
soil conditions, weather and local agricultural guidance.

Do not recommend fertilizers or pesticides solely
from an AI-generated guess.

Use verified soil tests and local expert guidance
before making chemical treatment decisions.


COTTON CROP - DISEASE KNOWLEDGE

1. COTTON BACTERIAL BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum (bacterium).

Possible symptoms:
- Angular, water-soaked leaf spots.
- Dark lesions along leaf veins.
- Lesions may expand and cause leaf blighting.
- Infection may also affect stems and bolls.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves, veins, stems and bolls.
Record lesion distribution and crop growth stage.

2. COTTON ALTERNARIA LEAF SPOT

Causal organism:
Alternaria species (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Brown circular or irregular leaf spots.
- Spots may develop concentric rings.
- Severely affected leaves may turn yellow and fall.

Monitoring:
Inspect older and lower leaves for expanding lesions.
Record the number and distribution of affected plants.

3. COTTON ANTHRACNOSE

Causal organism:
Colletotrichum species (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Dark lesions on stems and seedling tissues.
- Seedling blight and damping-off may occur.
- Bolls may develop dark, sunken lesions.
- Severe infection may cause boll damage.

Monitoring:
Inspect seedlings, stems and developing bolls.
Record affected plant parts and symptom progression.

4. COTTON FUSARIUM WILT

Causal organism:
Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. vasinfectum (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Yellowing and wilting of leaves.
- Stunted plant growth.
- Vascular browning may be visible when stems are cut.
- Symptoms may appear unevenly across the field.

Monitoring:
Inspect affected plants and compare them with healthy plants.
Record wilt distribution and soil conditions.
Seek expert confirmation before identifying the disease.

5. GENERAL DISEASE ADVISORY

Cotton diseases may produce similar symptoms.
Water stress, nutrient problems and root damage
can also cause leaf yellowing and wilting.

Inspect affected plant parts and record symptom progression.
Consider field history, crop stage and environmental conditions.

Do not confirm diseases from symptoms alone.
Seek laboratory or agricultural expert confirmation.

Do not prescribe fungicides solely from AI-generated findings.
Use locally approved agricultural recommendations.


MAIZE CROP - DISEASE KNOWLEDGE

1. TURCICUM LEAF BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Exserohilum turcicum (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Long, elliptical, grey-green to brown lesions on leaves.
- Lesions may enlarge and merge under favourable conditions.
- Severe infection can reduce healthy leaf area.

Monitoring:
Inspect lower and upper leaves for elongated lesions.
Record lesion size, distribution and crop growth stage.

2. MAYDIS LEAF BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Bipolaris maydis (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Small to elongated brown lesions on leaves.
- Lesions may become tan or brown as they develop.
- Severe infection can cause extensive leaf damage.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves for characteristic lesions.
Observe whether symptoms spread to additional leaves.

3. COMMON RUST

Causal organism:
Puccinia sorghi (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Small, raised, reddish-brown pustules on leaf surfaces.
- Pustules may appear on both leaf surfaces.
- Older pustules may become darker.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves for raised rust pustules.
Record their distribution and severity.

4. DOWNY MILDEW

Causal organisms:
Species of Peronosclerospora and related genera.

Possible symptoms:
- Pale chlorotic stripes on leaves.
- White downy growth may appear on affected leaf surfaces.
- Plants may become stunted.
- Some infections can cause abnormal tassel development.

Monitoring:
Inspect young plants for chlorotic striping and unusual growth.
Record affected plants and field distribution.
Seek expert confirmation because symptoms can vary by species.

5. GENERAL DISEASE ADVISORY

Leaf discoloration and lesions can have multiple causes.

Consider crop variety, weather, crop stage and field history.
Inspect affected leaves and monitor symptom progression.

Do not confirm a disease from symptoms alone.
Seek laboratory or agricultural expert confirmation
when identification is uncertain.

Do not prescribe fungicides solely from AI-generated findings.
Use locally approved agricultural recommendations.

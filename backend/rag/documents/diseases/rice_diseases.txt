
RICE CROP - DISEASE KNOWLEDGE

1. BLAST DISEASE

Causal organism:
Magnaporthe oryzae (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Spindle-shaped lesions on leaves.
- Lesions may have grey or whitish centres and darker margins.
- Neck infection may cause panicles to become partially or completely unfilled.

Favourable conditions:
Disease development is influenced by humidity,
temperature, crop variety and nitrogen management.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves, nodes and panicle necks.
Record lesion appearance and spread across the field.

2. BACTERIAL LEAF BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae (bacterium).

Possible symptoms:
- Yellowing and drying beginning near leaf tips or margins.
- Wavy lesions extending along leaf edges.
- Severe infection may cause extensive leaf drying.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaf margins and tips.
Observe whether lesions expand along the leaves.
Record affected field areas and crop stage.

3. BROWN SPOT

Causal organism:
Bipolaris oryzae (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Brown, circular or oval spots on leaves.
- Spots may have lighter centres and darker margins.
- Grain discoloration may occur in affected crops.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves and grains for characteristic spots.
Consider soil fertility and crop condition when assessing risk.

4. SHEATH BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Rhizoctonia solani (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Oval or irregular greenish-grey lesions on leaf sheaths.
- Lesions may develop near the waterline.
- Infection can spread to leaves and neighbouring tillers.

Monitoring:
Inspect lower leaf sheaths and plant bases.
Observe whether lesions spread upward through the canopy.

5. GENERAL DISEASE ADVISORY

Several rice diseases can produce similar symptoms.
Do not confirm a disease from one visual symptom alone.

Consider crop variety, growth stage, field moisture,
weather conditions and symptom distribution.

Record affected plant parts and monitor disease progression.
Seek expert or laboratory confirmation when necessary.

Do not recommend fungicides or bactericides solely
from AI-generated predictions.

Use locally approved agricultural guidance.

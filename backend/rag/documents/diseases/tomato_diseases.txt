
TOMATO CROP - DISEASE KNOWLEDGE

1. EARLY BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Alternaria solani (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Brown leaf spots with characteristic concentric rings.
- Yellowing around affected leaf areas.
- Older leaves are often affected first.
- Severe infection may cause premature leaf drying.

Monitoring:
Inspect lower and older leaves for expanding lesions.
Record lesion appearance and disease progression.

2. LATE BLIGHT

Causal organism:
Phytophthora infestans (oomycete).

Possible symptoms:
- Irregular water-soaked leaf lesions.
- Lesions may become brown or dark.
- White growth may appear on leaf undersides during humid conditions.
- Dark lesions may develop on stems.
- Fruits may develop firm, brown lesions.

Favourable conditions:
Cool temperatures and prolonged leaf wetness
can favour disease development.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves, stems and fruits regularly.
Pay attention to rapidly expanding lesions.
Record recent weather and symptom progression.

3. BACTERIAL WILT

Causal organism:
Ralstonia solanacearum species complex (bacterium).

Possible symptoms:
- Sudden wilting of green leaves.
- Plants may wilt without initial yellowing.
- Vascular tissues may become brown.
- Milky bacterial streaming may sometimes be observed
  from freshly cut stems placed in clean water.

Monitoring:
Inspect wilted plants and their stems.
Check soil moisture and drainage conditions.
Seek laboratory confirmation because other problems
can also cause wilting.

4. TOMATO LEAF CURL DISEASE

Causal agent:
Tomato leaf curl virus species, commonly transmitted
by whiteflies.

Possible symptoms:
- Upward or downward curling of leaves.
- Leaf yellowing and reduced leaf size.
- Shortened internodes.
- Stunted plant growth.
- Reduced fruit formation.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaf shape and plant growth.
Check for whitefly populations.
Record affected plants and their distribution.

5. SEPTORIA LEAF SPOT

Causal organism:
Septoria lycopersici (fungus).

Possible symptoms:
- Numerous small, circular leaf spots.
- Spots may have grey centres and dark margins.
- Tiny dark fruiting structures may appear within lesions.
- Severely affected leaves may turn yellow and fall.

Monitoring:
Inspect lower leaves for small characteristic lesions.
Record disease progression and field distribution.

6. GENERAL DISEASE ADVISORY

Several tomato diseases can produce similar symptoms.

Distinguish infectious diseases from:
- Nutrient deficiencies.
- Water stress.
- Root damage.
- Chemical injury.
- Environmental stress.

Record crop stage, affected plant parts,
weather conditions and symptom progression.

Do not confirm a disease from visual symptoms alone.
Seek laboratory or agricultural expert confirmation
when identification is uncertain.

Do not prescribe pesticides or fungicides solely
from AI-generated findings.

Use locally approved agricultural recommendations.

CROP IRRIGATION AND WATER MANAGEMENT GUIDE

1. IMPORTANCE OF IRRIGATION

Water is essential for plant growth, nutrient transport,
photosynthesis and crop development.

Insufficient water can cause wilting, reduced growth
and lower crop productivity.

Excessive irrigation can cause waterlogging,
root damage and nutrient leaching.

2. WEATHER AND RAINFALL

Irrigation decisions should consider:
- Current soil moisture
- Recent rainfall
- Expected rainfall
- Temperature
- Evapotranspiration
- Soil texture
- Crop growth stage

Forecast rainfall should not automatically be treated
as a substitute for irrigation.

Actual rainfall received and soil moisture should be
checked before making irrigation decisions.

3. CROP GROWTH STAGES

Water requirements vary throughout crop development.

Important wheat growth stages include:
- Crown root initiation
- Tillering
- Jointing
- Booting
- Flowering
- Grain filling

Water stress during critical growth stages may
negatively affect crop development and yield.

4. IRRIGATION SCHEDULING

Irrigation schedules should be based on crop requirements,
soil moisture, rainfall and local climate conditions.

A fixed watering schedule may not suit every farm.

Consider soil type and irrigation method before
estimating irrigation frequency or water quantity.

5. WATER STRESS

Possible signs of water stress include:
- Wilting
- Reduced leaf growth
- Dry soil
- Reduced plant development

These symptoms can also have other causes.
Confirm soil conditions before deciding to irrigate.

6. WATERLOGGING

Excessive soil water can reduce oxygen availability
around plant roots.

Poor drainage may contribute to root damage
and reduced crop health.

Inspect field drainage and avoid unnecessary irrigation.

7. FARM RECORDS

Maintain records of:
- Irrigation dates
- Estimated water quantity
- Rainfall received
- Soil moisture readings
- Crop growth stage
- Irrigation-related observations

Use historical records to improve future irrigation decisions.

8. ADVISORY SAFETY

Do not recommend a precise irrigation quantity without
sufficient information about soil, crop and field conditions.

Consider local agricultural recommendations and
available water resources before advising farmers.

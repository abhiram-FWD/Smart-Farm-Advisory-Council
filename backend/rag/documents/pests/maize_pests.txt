
MAIZE CROP - PEST KNOWLEDGE

1. FALL ARMYWORM

Scientific name:
Spodoptera frugiperda.

Common identification:
Fall armyworm larvae feed on maize leaves and may damage
the central whorl of young plants.

Possible symptoms:
- Window-pane feeding marks on young leaves.
- Irregular holes and ragged leaf margins.
- Sawdust-like frass inside the leaf whorl.
- Severe feeding damage to the growing point.

Identification clues:
Mature larvae may show an inverted Y-shaped marking
on the head and four dark spots arranged in a square
near the end of the body.

Monitoring:
Inspect the central whorl of maize plants.
Look for larvae, feeding damage and frass.
Record affected plants and crop growth stage.

2. MAIZE STEM BORER

Common identification:
Stem borer larvae feed inside maize stems and may
damage growing points and internal plant tissues.

Possible symptoms:
- Shot holes on leaves.
- Dead hearts in young plants.
- Boreholes and frass on stems.
- Weak or broken stems.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves and stems for feeding damage.
Split affected stems when necessary to check for larvae.
Record the distribution of damaged plants.

3. SHOOT FLY

Common identification:
Shoot fly larvae may damage the growing point
of young maize plants.

Possible symptoms:
- Drying of the central shoot.
- Dead-heart symptoms.
- Poor plant development.
- Damaged growing points.

Monitoring:
Inspect young plants and affected central shoots.
Record plant age and damage distribution.

4. GENERAL PEST ADVISORY

Leaf damage alone cannot confirm a particular pest.

Inspect larvae, feeding patterns, frass and damaged plant parts.
Record crop stage and infestation severity.

Distinguish insect damage from drought, nutrient problems
and mechanical injury.

Do not prescribe insecticides solely from AI predictions.
Use locally approved agricultural guidance.

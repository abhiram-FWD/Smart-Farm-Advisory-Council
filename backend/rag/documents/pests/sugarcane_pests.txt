
SUGARCANE CROP - PEST KNOWLEDGE

1. EARLY SHOOT BORER

Scientific name:
Chilo infuscatellus.

Possible symptoms:
- Dead hearts in young sugarcane shoots.
- Central leaves may dry while outer leaves remain green.
- Bore holes may be visible near the shoot base.
- Damaged shoots may produce an unpleasant smell.

Monitoring:
Inspect young shoots and the base of affected plants.
Record the number and distribution of dead hearts.

2. TOP BORER

Scientific name:
Scirpophaga excerptalis.

Possible symptoms:
- Boring damage near the growing point.
- Dead heart symptoms in affected shoots.
- Characteristic parallel rows of shot holes may appear
  on leaves after the damaged leaf unfolds.
- Top growth may become distorted.

Monitoring:
Inspect the central spindle and young leaves.
Look for feeding damage and shot-hole patterns.

3. SUGARCANE INTERNODE BORER

Scientific name:
Chilo sacchariphagus indicus.

Possible symptoms:
- Larvae bore into sugarcane internodes.
- Small entry holes may be visible on the stalk.
- Internal tunnelling may damage the cane.
- Infested internodes may show reddish discolouration.

Monitoring:
Inspect stalks for entry holes and internal damage.
Record the affected plants and growth stage.

4. SUGARCANE WHITEFLY

Scientific name:
Aleurolobus barodensis.

Possible symptoms:
- Whitefly colonies on the undersides of leaves.
- Yellowing and weakening of affected leaves.
- Honeydew and sooty mould may develop.
- Severe infestation may reduce plant vigour.

Monitoring:
Inspect the undersides of leaves.
Record whitefly abundance and visible leaf damage.

5. SUGARCANE PYRILLA

Scientific name:
Pyrilla perpusilla.

Possible symptoms:
- Sap-sucking insects on leaf undersides.
- Yellowing and drying of leaves.
- Sticky honeydew deposits.
- Black sooty mould may develop on leaf surfaces.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaf undersides for insects and nymphs.
Record infestation levels and crop condition.

6. GENERAL PEST ADVISORY

Pest symptoms can overlap with disease and environmental
stress symptoms.

Record:
- Crop age and growth stage.
- Affected plant parts.
- Visible insects, larvae or feeding damage.
- Distribution of affected plants.
- Recent field observations.

Do not confirm pest identity from symptoms alone
when the insect cannot be identified.

Do not prescribe pesticides solely from AI-generated findings.
Use locally approved integrated pest management guidance
and consult an agricultural expert when necessary.


TOMATO CROP - PEST KNOWLEDGE

1. TOMATO FRUIT BORER

Scientific name:
Helicoverpa armigera.

Common identification:
Larvae feed on tomato flowers, leaves and developing fruits.

Possible symptoms:
- Circular holes in tomato fruits.
- Larvae may partially enter fruits and feed internally.
- Damaged fruits may rot due to secondary infection.
- Flowers and young fruits may be damaged.

Monitoring:
Inspect flowers and developing fruits regularly.
Look for larvae, entry holes and feeding damage.
Record affected fruits and crop growth stage.

2. WHITEFLY

Scientific name:
Bemisia tabaci.

Common identification:
Small white insects usually found on the undersides of leaves.

Possible symptoms:
- Yellowing of leaves.
- Sticky honeydew and sooty mould.
- Reduced plant vigour.
- Whiteflies can transmit certain plant viruses.

Monitoring:
Inspect the undersides of leaves.
Observe adult insects and immature stages.
Record insect populations and unusual leaf symptoms.

3. TOMATO LEAF MINER

Scientific name:
Tuta absoluta.

Common identification:
Larvae feed within leaves, stems and fruits.

Possible symptoms:
- Irregular mines inside leaves.
- Blotchy or tunnel-like feeding marks.
- Damaged shoots and growing points.
- Small holes in fruits.

Monitoring:
Inspect leaves for mines and larvae.
Check stems and fruits for feeding damage.
Record the distribution of affected plants.

4. APHIDS

Common identification:
Small, soft-bodied insects occurring in colonies
on tender shoots and leaf undersides.

Possible symptoms:
- Curled or distorted leaves.
- Sticky honeydew.
- Reduced growth in heavily affected plants.
- Some aphid species can transmit plant viruses.

Monitoring:
Inspect young shoots and leaf undersides.
Record colony size and plant condition.

5. GENERAL PEST ADVISORY

Pest damage may resemble disease symptoms or
environmental stress.

Inspect insects, feeding marks and damaged plant parts
before identifying a suspected pest.

Record crop stage, infestation distribution
and observed damage.

Do not recommend insecticides solely from AI predictions.
Use locally approved agricultural guidance.

SOIL MANAGEMENT AND AGRONOMY GUIDE

1. SOIL pH

Soil pH affects nutrient availability and microbial activity.
Different crops have different preferred soil pH ranges.

Highly acidic or alkaline soil conditions can reduce the
availability of certain nutrients.

Soil pH should be measured using a reliable soil testing method.
Do not recommend lime or other amendments without considering
soil test results and local agricultural guidance.

2. SOIL MOISTURE

Soil moisture affects root development, nutrient uptake,
photosynthesis and overall crop growth.

Low soil moisture may cause water stress and wilting.
Excessive moisture may cause waterlogging and poor root aeration.

Soil moisture should be interpreted according to soil texture,
crop type, growth stage and recent weather conditions.

3. MACRONUTRIENTS

Nitrogen supports vegetative growth and chlorophyll production.
Phosphorus supports root development and energy transfer.
Potassium supports water regulation and stress tolerance.

Nutrient deficiencies may affect crop growth and productivity.
Visual symptoms alone cannot confirm a deficiency.

4. SOIL TESTING

Soil testing helps determine nutrient availability and pH.

A soil report may include:
- Soil pH
- Electrical conductivity
- Organic carbon
- Available nitrogen
- Available phosphorus
- Available potassium

Fertilizer recommendations should consider crop requirements,
soil test results and regional agricultural recommendations.

5. SOIL HEALTH MANAGEMENT

Organic matter contributes to soil structure and water retention.

Crop rotation, appropriate residue management and
balanced nutrient management can support soil health.

Avoid excessive fertilizer application.
Monitor soil conditions regularly and maintain farm records.

6. ADVISORY SAFETY

Do not diagnose a nutrient deficiency solely from leaf colour.
Use laboratory soil reports and crop-specific guidance.

When soil measurements are unavailable or unclear,
recommend soil testing rather than inventing nutrient values.
